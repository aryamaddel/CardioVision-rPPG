A HEARTBEAT-BASED DEEPFAKE DETECTOR

Fakes can copy a face.
Not the heartbeat under it.

Real faces faintly change colour with every heartbeat — camera light sees it. AI face swaps look flawless but skip the blood flow. We tested 54 video clips — 44 real people, 10 AI fakes — and checked one question: do all parts of the face pulse together?

01 / HOW IT WORKS

Four simple steps.

No special hardware — one normal camera
01

Watch the face

Track the face in every frame and split it into 3 zones: forehead, left cheek, right cheek.

02

Read the wiggles

Each zone's colour wiggles a tiny bit with every heartbeat. Turn those wiggles into a pulse signal.

03

Check two things

Pulse strength — is there a clear heartbeat rhythm?
Teamwork — do all zones show the same pulse?

04

Decide

REAL if the pulse is strong, all zones agree, and the heart rate is human (45–140 BPM).

IS ANYTHING TRAINED? RULE-BASED CORE = NO; A SMALL NEURAL VARIANT = YES
NO TRAINING

The main detector learns nothing

The whole detector is a fixed formula set: the POS colour-to-pulse math (chosen button, Wang et al. 2017) plus simple thresholds. Nothing is fitted — which also means overfitting is impossible here.

PRETRAINED

Face models used as-is

Face tracking (MediaPipe / SCRFD + mesh) and the AI-swap generator (inswapper_128 + ArcFace) come pretrained from their authors. We only configured them.

TRAINED · INT8 MLP

Small neural variant (spin-off)

A spin-off detector is trained: same POS pulse as input, but a learned 36-feature → INT8 MLP decides REAL vs AI. See Trained model. The threshold rules above remain the no-training core.

05 / A TRAINED DETECTOR

The same pulse, learned instead of ruled.

INT8 · runs anywhere · 4 KB
276

training windows — 138 real (44 UBFC subjects) vs 138 AI, cut into exactly equal 50/50 folds. Every 12 s window becomes 36 numbers: pulse strength per face zone, zone-to-zone teamwork, heart rate, plus 24 spectral shape bins. A tiny neural net (36 → 24 → 2) learns to separate the classes, then ships quantized to INT8 — the whole model is 4 KB, no GPU needed at inference.

The AI side is deliberately harder than the face-swap fakes above: 24 fully generated clips — diffusion talking faces (EMO / EMO2, with AI-generated source portraits) plus AI-themed stock footage (laser face-scan shots, a brush-painted face, head turns). Provenance for every clip is listed in tmp/ai_pool/manifest.json.

Why INT8 only. During validation the INT8 model matched the full-precision reference (0.835 vs 0.823 window accuracy, same identity accuracy and AUC — quantization noise even trimmed the fold spread), so the ladder was reduced to INT8: one artifact, 4 KB, CPU-cheap, no FP16/FP32 export to maintain. The weights live in the release ↗.

Complement, not replacement. The threshold rules (section 01) run with zero training and won't overfit by construction; the MLP learns the same physiology on a much harder AI pool and can weigh borderline cases. They share the identical pulse-extraction front end, so both run from one pass over the video.

In one sentence: both catch every fake — but the GPU's real/fake groups sit further apart, so its verdicts survive worse video. It also runs everything on one card. The CPU is the faster, more precise point-tracker; the GPU is the better detector.

Why the margin matters. A detector with a thin margin works only on clean studio videos. The GPU's wider gap between real and fake means the same rule keeps working on compressed, shaky, short clips — like the ones real applications would see.

Honest limitations

  • Small first study. The 12-clip test covers 2 people; one missed real would shift accuracy a lot. The 44-person test is the better evidence.
  • Head movement can fake teamwork. A clip whose head bobs on the beat can pass — the classic way to trick this test.
  • Small clips lose detail. The 42 extra subjects come as 128×128 compressed clips, so their teamwork scores are lower and the "real" line had to move down.

Synthetic fakes are generated from the dataset for research only.
