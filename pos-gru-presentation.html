04 · Hypotheses

Four falsifiable claims — and how we judge them

H1

POS-GRU beats POS when motion or illumination changes.

H2

Feeding POS as input beats RGB-only GRU.

H3

On stable video, POS-GRU matches POS — no degradation.

H4

A compact GRU meets real-time on target hardware.

05 · Reference in context

Wang et al. 2017 — POS (our baseline)

The paper

Algorithmic Principles of Remote PPG, IEEE Transactions on Biomedical Engineering, 2017. Formalises Plane-Orthogonal-to-Skin (POS) as a chrominance method for rPPG: build a colour plane orthogonal to skin tone in normalized RGB, project the skin trace onto it, and read out the pulse.

RGB trace
→
orthogonal colour plane
→
pulse signal

Why it matters to our hypothesis

  • POS is the interpretable baseline and fallback in all three inference modes
  • Its signal is our input feature — we preserve the physics, no new physiology
  • H3 assumes POS is reliable on stable video — this paper is the evidence we test against
  • Any POS-GRU gain is measured relative to this method
06 · Reference in context

RhythmNet 2020 — GRU on rPPG (our precedent)

The paper

RhythmNet: A Versatile Network for Remote Heart Rate Estimation from Videos, IJCAI 2020 (Niu et al.). An end-to-end network that extracts a spatial–temporal representation from face video, then uses a time-series network built on GRU to model the temporal dependencies of the heart-rate signal.

face video
→
spatio-temporal CNN
→
GRU
→
HR sequence

Why it matters to our hypothesis

  • Direct evidence that a GRU over temporal rPPG features works — supports our H4 real-time & GRU-over-CNN choice
  • Their GRU consumes learned CNN features — our H2 asks whether the POS physics can replace that learned front-end
  • If POS features match CNN features (H2), we get more data-efficient training
  • Same waveform→HR output structure: GRU refines, BPM stays downstream
07 · Reference in context

DeepPhys 2018 — the case for a hybrid (our contrast)

The paper

DeepPhys: Video-Based Physiological Measurement Using Convolutional Attention Networks, WACV 2018 (Chen & McDuff). An end-to-end two-branch CNN — a motion branch and an appearance branch with attention — that learns rPPG directly from pixels and can beat classical methods.

video frames
→
motion CNN + attention
→
pulse

Why it matters to our hypothesis

  • Shows learned rPPG can outperform POS — but needs large datasets and compute
  • Motivates our middle path: keep POS as inductive bias, learn only a small temporal stage
  • Their CNN is spatial; our corruption is temporal — GRU targets the failure mode
  • Their black-box pulse trades interpretability — we keep BPM estimation downstream
